# DAY I — The Hand That Makes It Real

A pale field of amendments appeared beneath Elias's signature while Mara's supervisor was still speaking.

It had been empty when Elias left. The transfer itself was already irrevocable and inside the railway system. What remained was the final release of the reassigned passage—the issuance of the standby ticket, after which the railway could neither withhold it nor attach another condition.

On the platform beyond the glass, the last mail cage was being rolled toward the through carriage.

“Don't validate yet,” her supervisor said. “Communications has added standard protections.”

Mara read them on the portable signature tablet. The recipient would agree to an interview after boarding. The railway could use the recipient's name, image, and account of the journey. If participation was withdrawn, the railway could cancel the passage and choose another passenger.

Nothing in the language called itself payment.

“The transfer says no consideration,” Mara said.

“There is no consideration. We aren't charging them.”

“If they refuse the interview, do they keep the berth?”

There was a short silence on the line.

“The situation would be reviewed.”

“Then the interview is part of the fare.”

Her supervisor lowered his voice. He was not an unreasonable man. The station had been accused before of inventing acts of generosity for publicity. An interview would establish that a real passenger had received the passage. A release would prevent disputes. A cancellation clause would protect the railway if the facts later proved embarrassing.

“We can make this useful,” he said. “Otherwise we have an anonymous transaction nobody can verify and a story nobody can tell.”

At the end of the counter, Ivo waited without approaching. He was a traveling editor for Enoch, the independent archive that had received the terms-only submission. His place on the departing circuit was already confirmed. He had come to collect the final validation before boarding.

Mara covered the tablet with her palm.

“What do you need?” she asked him.

“The terms that took effect.”

“The passenger's name?”

“No.”

“A statement from Elias?”

“No.”

“How the journey is used?”

Ivo looked toward the waiting carriage, then back at the covered screen.

“That isn't a term.”

The platform signal changed. Boarding staff began drawing the movable barriers together.

Her supervisor heard the station announcement through the line. “Mara, if you complete this without the additions, we lose access. We lose the right to correct it. Communications won't own any part of what follows.”

“They don't.”

She uncovered the tablet.

The first amendment was longer than the original transfer instruction. Mara selected it and removed it. The interview field disappeared with it. She removed the release, including the sentence that extended permission to future formats not yet known. Last, she deleted the cancellation clause.

The system asked whether the omissions were intentional.

She confirmed that they were.

The final screen contained less language than the provisional one. It stated that the passage had been transferred for no payment or consideration. It required no interview, release, contact, or account of use. It reserved no cancellation power connected to the recipient's identity, conduct, or willingness to participate in publicity.

A warning appeared beneath the record: after validation, no additional condition could be attached.

Mara signed.

Then she slid the tablet from the railway's side of the desk to the independent witness's side.

Ivo did not ask who had been selected. He read the operative terms, checked the timestamp against the station clock, and signed only as witness to the record. The tablet registered the validation as final.

Behind the glass, a printer inside the gate enclosure released the new boarding document. Mara turned the tablet face down before the passenger field could refresh on her side.

Ivo returned the tablet and reached the through carriage before its doors closed. The train began to move with no announcement beyond its ordinary destination sequence.

“You will have to explain why you removed our safeguards,” her supervisor said.

Mara watched the last carriage pass the end of the platform.

“They safeguarded our ability to take the gift back.”

The call ended.

Enoch preserved the final validation record: no consideration, no release requirement, no retained route of cancellation. It preserved no recipient biography because none had been necessary to the decision.

Before the train cleared the terminal roof, an editorial request reached Ivo's queue: find the passenger, explain why the passage mattered, and give the readers someone they could believe deserved it.

He read the request twice. The record beside it offered no answer.
