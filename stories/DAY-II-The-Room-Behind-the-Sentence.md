# DAY II — The Room Behind the Sentence

The e-ink reader kept the empty fields visible as pale rectangles.

Ivo had removed the questions, but their former positions remained: passenger name, age, occupation, reason for travel, photograph, statement. The page looked less unfinished than ventilated, as if the missing answers allowed air to pass through it.

The train had cleared the terminal district. Warehouses and apartment blocks moved across the window, their upper floors brightening before the streets below. In the work compartment, the wheels made a low continuous sound beneath the tables.

The publisher that carried Enoch's public notices sent the draft back.

**We need the person.**

Another message followed before Ivo could answer.

**Not necessarily the name. Give us enough to understand why the passage mattered.**

He read the request on the same reader that held the final validation record. The railway had transferred a paid berth through its ordinary standby system. No payment had been required from the recipient. No interview, release, contact, publicity, conduct, gratitude, or account of use had been attached. The transfer was complete.

The facts occupied a few short paragraphs. The empty fields occupied nearly half the page.

The publisher's editor called.

“Nobody will believe a clean record if there is no life inside it,” she said.

“There is a passenger.”

“Then let the reader meet them.”

“The railway chose through the ordinary standby allocation. The choice was not based on a story.”

“Every choice has a story.”

Ivo looked through the glass wall of the compartment. At the next table, Salma had spread a folded deed beside a thermos. She was marking clauses in pencil while the city thinned into service roads, drainage channels, and fenced yards.

The editor softened her voice. She asked for no spectacle. A few true details would be enough: someone visiting a sick parent, someone beginning again, someone who could never otherwise have afforded the circuit. Without that human measure, she said, the piece would make the railway appear careless and the gift arbitrary.

“What if none of those things is true?” Ivo asked.

“Find what is true.”

He could have done it. The boarding document existed inside the railway system. A station employee could describe the person without naming them. A porter might remember a coat, a suitcase, the way a hand shook when the ticket was received. From ordinary fragments he could build a life that no single source had disclosed.

It would be accurate in pieces and false in purpose.

The train entered a cutting between concrete walls. The compartment darkened, and the reader adjusted its surface without shining. Ivo opened the publication controls.

The publisher's desk had restored fields headed **circumstances**, **need**, and **likely impact**. None was required by the validation record. Each invited the recipient to earn, after the fact, what had already been given.

Ivo deleted them again.

“Then what are we publishing?” the editor asked.

“What happened.”

“That is a transaction note.”

“It is the only account that doesn't purchase meaning from the passenger.”

At the end of the compartment stood a translucent acoustic partition. It enclosed a narrow review space without hiding the shape of the person inside. Ivo carried the e-ink reader there and stepped behind it. The conversation from the tables became indistinct, but the page in his hands remained exact.

He read the proof once more.

It stated what the giver had relinquished and what the recipient did not owe. It did not say the passage had saved a life, rewarded virtue, repaired an injustice, or produced a measurable good. Where identity would ordinarily establish worthiness, the fields were marked **intentionally omitted**.

Ivo selected **publish as final record**.

The system warned that later biographical additions would appear as separate commentary and could not be merged into the proof. He approved.

When he stepped back through the partition, the industrial yards had given way to allotments and strips of wet grass. Salma was reading the account on her phone. Her deed lay open beneath one hand.

“You left out the reason,” she said.

“It wasn't a condition of the passage.”

She turned the deed toward him. A paragraph near the bottom allowed land given for a public greenhouse to return to the donor if future gardeners failed to maintain the place according to her judgment.

“I called this protection,” she said.

The train began slowing for the long agricultural stop. Beyond the window, the greenhouse roof caught the morning light in dull rectangular panes.

Ivo did not tell her what to remove. Enoch preserved the published text and a record of the identity fields that had been deliberately omitted. Salma read her paragraph again while the platform drew alongside.

*What grows after its owner stops returning?*
